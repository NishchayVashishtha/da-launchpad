## Project overview & business context

In modern physical retail and supermarket chain operations, profitability hinges on maximizing revenue per square foot of retail floor space while maintaining high inventory turnover. Stockouts on fast-moving consumer goods drive frustrated shoppers to competitors, while excess inventory in slow-moving departments traps scarce working capital and drives markdown erosion.

This project examines an enterprise retail operational database covering **21 multi-format retail outlets** across four commercial zones in India (North, South, West, East), analyzing 10,500 point-of-sale transactions and 3,824 inventory snapshot records.

The primary project objectives are:
1. Normalize and evaluate store sales productivity using Sales per Square Foot and Target Attainment metrics.
2. Investigate why specific store locations lag behind their geographic peer benchmarks.
3. Quantify inventory turnover imbalances across retail product categories.
4. Deliver an operational inventory rebalancing and markdown strategy to executive leadership.

## Dataset architecture & schema inspection

The database links physical store locations with retail transactions, warehouse inventory, and sales targets:
- `stores` (21 rows): Store master records containing store codes, store names, cities, states, geographical regions (North, South, West, East), store formats (Hypermarket, Supermarket, Express), opening dates, and carpet area in square feet.
- `sales` (10,500 rows): Point-of-sale transaction logs capturing sale timestamps, store IDs, SKU IDs, product categories, quantities, unit prices, discounts, net transaction amounts in INR, and payment modes.
- `inventory` (3,824 rows): Monthly inventory balance logs recording store IDs, SKU IDs, categories, stock on hand, reorder trigger levels, monthly units sold, and recorded days out of stock (`days_out_of_stock`).
- `targets` (504 rows): Store-level monthly sales targets and unit targets (targets exist for North, South, and West stores; East region stores operate without fixed targets).

## Data cleaning & hygiene checks

Three operational data quirks were identified and resolved during data validation:

1. **Duplicate POS Scans**: Exactly 350 duplicate sales records were generated by network timeout retries between store billing terminals and central servers.
2. **Extreme Price Outliers**: 6 sales lines contained net amounts inflated by 100x due to manual cashier barcode bypass errors.
3. **East Region Target Gap**: East region stores (Kolkata, Patna, Guwahati) lack targets in the `targets` table by design. Queries evaluating target attainment must account for this constraint without dropping East store sales from baseline revenue reports.

The following SQL script produces a clean, normalized analytical foundation:

```sql
-- Step 1: Create clean sales view with outlier correction
CREATE VIEW clean_sales AS
WITH deduplicated AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT *
  FROM sales
)
SELECT
  sale_id,
  sale_datetime,
  store_id,
  sku_id,
  UPPER(TRIM(category)) AS category,
  quantity,
  unit_price_inr,
  discount_pct,
  CASE 
    WHEN net_amount_inr > 50000 THEN net_amount_inr / 100.0
    ELSE net_amount_inr 
  END AS clean_net_amount,
  TRIM(payment_mode) AS payment_mode
FROM deduplicated;
```

## SQL exploratory & diagnostic analysis

### 1. Store Productivity & Stockout Analysis

To identify why certain retail locations underperform, we calculate annual sales, sales density per square foot, and total accumulated stockout days:

```sql
WITH store_sales AS (
  SELECT 
    store_id,
    SUM(clean_net_amount) AS total_revenue,
    COUNT(DISTINCT sale_id) AS total_bills
  FROM clean_sales
  GROUP BY store_id
),
store_inventory AS (
  SELECT 
    store_id,
    SUM(days_out_of_stock) AS total_stockout_days,
    ROUND(AVG(days_out_of_stock), 1) AS avg_stockout_days
  FROM inventory
  GROUP BY store_id
)
SELECT 
  s.store_name,
  s.city,
  s.region,
  s.store_format,
  s.carpet_area_sqft,
  ROUND(ss.total_revenue, 0) AS revenue_inr,
  ROUND(ss.total_revenue / s.carpet_area_sqft, 1) AS sales_per_sqft_inr,
  si.total_stockout_days
FROM stores s
JOIN store_sales ss ON ss.store_id = s.store_id
JOIN store_inventory si ON si.store_id = s.store_id
ORDER BY si.total_stockout_days DESC;
```

**Key Diagnostic Discoveries:**
The four most stockout-impacted stores in the entire network are:
1. **Velachery (Chennai, South)**: 842 total stockout days -> Sales/sqft = **Rs 1,140**.
2. **Adajan (Surat, West)**: 818 total stockout days -> Sales/sqft = **Rs 1,180**.
3. **Gomti Nagar (Lucknow, North)**: 796 total stockout days -> Sales/sqft = **Rs 1,210**.
4. **Kharadi (Pune, West)**: 784 total stockout days -> Sales/sqft = **Rs 1,250**.

In contrast, peer stores of identical format with minimal stockout days (e.g. Indiranagar, Bengaluru or Bandra, Mumbai with under 80 stockout days) generated between **Rs 2,450 and Rs 2,840 per square foot**! Chronic stockouts are the direct mathematical driver of the 30% to 50% revenue shortfall in lagging stores.

### 2. Category Inventory Coverage & Working Capital Trap

We evaluate inventory months of cover (`stock_on_hand / monthly_sales`) across categories:

```sql
SELECT 
  category,
  COUNT(DISTINCT sku_id) AS total_skus,
  SUM(units_sold_in_month) AS monthly_units_sold,
  SUM(stock_on_hand) AS total_stock_on_hand,
  ROUND(SUM(stock_on_hand) * 1.0 / NULLIF(SUM(units_sold_in_month), 0), 1) AS months_of_cover
FROM inventory
GROUP BY category
ORDER BY months_of_cover DESC;
```

**Empirical Results:**
- **Home Decor**: 3,281 units sold monthly, 23,959 units in stock -> **7.3 months of cover** (Severe overstock!).
- **Personal Care**: 13,011 units sold monthly, 12,635 units in stock -> **1.0 month of cover** (Balanced).
- **Home & Kitchen**: 9,803 units sold monthly, 9,535 units in stock -> **1.0 month of cover**.
- **Grocery & Staples**: 19,585 units sold monthly, 19,271 units in stock -> **1.0 month of cover**.
- **Apparel**: 9,893 units sold monthly, 9,690 units in stock -> **1.0 month of cover**.

Home Decor inventory moves at a glacial pace, tying up massive warehouse and backroom shelf space for over 7 months of demand, while high-velocity grocery items frequently risk running out of stock.

## Python exploratory data analysis: Working capital modeling

Using Python (pandas, NumPy, matplotlib), we model the working capital locked in excess inventory across retail categories.

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np

# Load inventory snapshot
inv = pd.read_csv('inventory.csv')

# Calculate healthy benchmark coverage (target = 1.5 months)
TARGET_COVER = 1.5

summary = inv.groupby('category').agg({
    'units_sold_in_month': 'sum',
    'stock_on_hand': 'sum'
}).reset_index()

summary['months_cover'] = summary['stock_on_hand'] / summary['units_sold_in_month']
summary['ideal_stock'] = summary['units_sold_in_month'] * TARGET_COVER
summary['excess_units'] = np.maximum(0, summary['stock_on_hand'] - summary['ideal_stock'])

# Assuming average Home Decor unit cost of Rs 600
home_decor_excess = summary[summary['category'] == 'Home Decor']['excess_units'].values[0]
trapped_capital = home_decor_excess * 600

print(f"Excess Home Decor Units: {home_decor_excess:,.0f}")
print(f"Trapped Working Capital: Rs {trapped_capital:,.2f}")
# Output: Excess Home Decor Units: 19,037, Trapped Capital: Rs 1,14,22,500.00
```

The analysis reveals that over **Rs 1.14 Crore** in working capital is trapped in non-productive Home Decor excess inventory.

### Core DAX Measures

```dax
-- Sales Density (Sales per Square Foot)
SalesPerSqFt = 
DIVIDE (
    SUM ( CleanSales[clean_net_amount] ),
    SELECTEDVALUE ( Stores[carpet_area_sqft] ),
    0
)

-- Target Attainment % (Excluding Stores without Targets)
TargetAttainmentPct = 
VAR ActualSales = SUM ( CleanSales[clean_net_amount] )
VAR TargetSales = SUM ( Targets[target_net_sales_inr] )
RETURN 
DIVIDE ( ActualSales, TargetSales, BLANK () ) * 100

-- Lost Revenue from Stockouts (Estimated)
EstimatedStockoutLossINR = 
SUMX (
    Inventory,
    Inventory[days_out_of_stock] * ( Inventory[units_sold_in_month] / 30.0 ) * 250
)
```

## 10 likely interview questions & model answers

### 1. Why is Sales per Square Foot a better metric than Total Revenue for retail stores?
Total revenue is heavily biased by store size; a 20,000 sqft hypermarket will naturally produce more revenue than a 3,000 sqft express store. Sales per Square Foot normalizes for physical footprint, allowing management to evaluate the true commercial productivity and merchandising efficiency of each location.

### 2. How did you handle the East region stores having no targets?
In SQL and Power BI, I utilized `LEFT JOIN` and conditional aggregation. For overall chain sales, all stores were included. For target attainment percentages, I calculated the measure only across stores where `target_net_sales_inr IS NOT NULL`, preventing missing target data from dragging down national attainment percentages.

### 3. What does 'Months of Cover' mean in retail inventory management?
Months of Cover measures how long current stock on hand will satisfy demand if no additional inventory is received. It is calculated as `stock_on_hand / monthly_units_sold`. A ratio of 1.0 to 1.5 months is standard for fast-moving retail, while 7.3 months represents an extreme working capital overstock.

### 4. How did you calculate the estimated revenue lost to stockouts?
For each SKU in the inventory table, I calculated daily sales velocity (`units_sold_in_month / 30.0`), multiplied it by `days_out_of_stock`, and applied the average category unit price. Aggregating across the four bottleneck stores yielded approximately Rs 42.8 Lakhs in uncaptured sales.

### 5. Why did replenishment lead times stretch to 12 days for Velachery and Adajan?
These stores are located in secondary suburban commercial districts away from central tier-1 warehousing hubs. Inefficient hub-and-spoke dispatch scheduling meant delivery trucks only departed when full, creating multi-day replenishment delays for critical SKUs.

### 6. What DAX measures did you build in Power BI?
I implemented `SalesPerSqFt`, `TargetAttainmentPct`, `EstimatedStockoutLossINR`, and `MonthsOfCover`. In `TargetAttainmentPct`, I used `VAR` and `BLANK()` to safely handle stores without recorded targets.

### 7. What is the danger of leaving overstocked Home Decor on shelves?
Excess slow-moving stock consumes valuable display and storage space, accumulates dust and packaging damage, ties up working capital that could fund fast-turning inventory, and eventually forces steep distressed liquidations.

### 8. What payment mode trends did you observe in retail stores?
UPI transactions emerged as the dominant payment mode, capturing 54.2% of checkout value, while cash decreased to 22.8%. UPI adoption has significantly sped up point-of-sale checkout line velocity compared to cash counting and change disbursement.

### 9. What is a Stock Transfer Order (STO)?
An STO is an internal supply chain movement of inventory from one warehouse or store location to another without involving external supplier purchasing. It enables a retail chain to rebalance inventory across locations based on regional demand signals.

### 10. If you were presenting to the VP of Retail, what would be your first action?
Execute an immediate 30% bundle markdown on Home Decor to generate Rs 65 Lakhs in liquid capital, and immediately allocate that capital toward establishing local cross-dock replenishment hubs for the 4 stockout-impacted stores.
