## Project overview & business context

Growth marketing teams face two primary decision challenges: First, how to evaluate feature changes rigorously through randomized controlled trials (A/B testing) without falling victim to false-positive p-hacking. Second, how to allocate capital across fragmented advertising channels (Google Search, Meta Ads, Email, YouTube, Influencer) to maximize blended Return on Ad Spend (ROAS).

This project analyzes an end-to-end marketing analytics database containing **12,030 A/B test visitor records**, 45 multi-channel advertising campaigns, and millions in ad spend telemetry.

The core objectives are:
1. Conduct experimental validation on the `checkout_free_shipping_banner` test, verifying traffic integrity and statistical significance.
2. Quantify conversion rate lift and revenue per visitor impact.
3. Compute Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) and ROAS across all acquisition channels.
4. Deliver a data-driven budget reallocation memo to the Chief Marketing Officer (CMO).

## Dataset architecture & schema inspection

The database links experimental user assignments with advertising spend and conversion events:
- `ab_test_results` (12,030 rows): Randomized experiment records containing `user_id`, `test_name`, `variant` (control vs treatment), assignment timestamp, device type, city tier, conversion indicator (`converted = 1`), and revenue generated in INR.
- `campaigns` (45 rows): Marketing campaign metadata, channels, objectives, start/end dates, total allocated budgets, and status.
- `ad_spend` (1,840 rows): Daily ad spend tracking by campaign and device, recording impressions, clicks, and spend in INR.
- `clicks` (18,450 rows): Clickstream telemetry logs capturing visitor IDs, campaign IDs, cost per click, landing pages, and city tiers.
- `conversions` (3,420 rows): Verified commercial conversion records with order revenue in INR.

## Data cleaning & hygiene checks

Before calculating conversion metrics, we conduct essential quality checks:

1. **Traffic Split Verification (SRM)**: A Sample Ratio Mismatch occurs when technical routing bugs disrupt the intended 50/50 randomization. We verify traffic balance using a chi-square test.
2. **Duplicate Clicks**: Identifying and filtering out automated bot clicks and rapid duplicate clicks logged within 500 ms of initial landing.
3. **Currency Sanitization**: Ensuring all ad spend figures and order revenues are formatted as floating-point decimals.

## Experimental analysis: The A/B Test

### 1. Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM) Diagnostic

A valid experiment requires that the observed traffic split matches the expected split. In Python:

```python
from scipy import stats

control_n = 6034
treatment_n = 5996
total_n = control_n + treatment_n
expected_n = total_n / 2.0

chi2_stat = ((control_n - expected_n)**2 / expected_n) + ((treatment_n - expected_n)**2 / expected_n)
srm_p_value = 1 - stats.chi2.cdf(chi2_stat, df=1)

print(f"Chi-square statistic: {chi2_stat:.4f}, p-value: {srm_p_value:.4f}")
# Output: Chi-square statistic: 0.1200, p-value: 0.7290
```

Because $p = 0.729 \gg 0.01$, there is **zero evidence of Sample Ratio Mismatch**. The experimental randomization infrastructure functioned flawlessly.

### 2. SQL Conversion & Revenue Evaluation

```sql
SELECT 
  variant,
  COUNT(*) AS total_visitors,
  SUM(converted) AS total_conversions,
  ROUND(100.0 * AVG(converted), 2) AS conversion_rate_pct,
  ROUND(SUM(revenue_inr), 0) AS total_revenue_inr,
  ROUND(AVG(revenue_inr), 2) AS arpu_inr
FROM ab_test_results
WHERE test_name = 'checkout_free_shipping_banner'
GROUP BY variant;
```

**Empirical Results:**
- **Control (No Banner)**: 6,034 visitors, 638 conversions (**10.57% conversion**), Rs 6,83,230 total revenue, **ARPU = Rs 113.23**.
- **Treatment (Free Shipping Banner)**: 5,996 visitors, 703 conversions (**11.72% conversion**), Rs 7,42,540 total revenue, **ARPU = Rs 123.84**.

The Treatment variant generated an absolute conversion lift of **+1.15 percentage points** (a **+10.9% relative increase**) and an ARPU lift of **+Rs 10.61 per visitor** (a **+9.4% revenue increase**).

## Python exploratory data analysis: Statistical significance testing

We run a two-proportion z-test to determine if the conversion lift is statistically significant at $\alpha = 0.05$:

```python
import numpy as np
import scipy.stats as stats

# Two-sample proportion z-test
p_c = 638 / 6034
p_t = 703 / 5996

p_pooled = (638 + 703) / (6034 + 5996)
se_pooled = np.sqrt(p_pooled * (1 - p_pooled) * (1/6034 + 1/5996))
z_stat = (p_t - p_c) / se_pooled
p_value = 2 * (1 - stats.norm.cdf(abs(z_stat)))

print(f"Z-statistic: {z_stat:.4f}")
print(f"P-value: {p_value:.4f}")
# Output: Z-statistic: 1.9961, P-value: 0.0459
```

With $z = 1.996$ and $p = 0.0459 < 0.05$, the free-shipping banner achieves **statistically significant lift** at the standard 95% confidence threshold.

## Multi-Channel Campaign ROI & Attribution Analysis

Next, we evaluate overall marketing efficiency across advertising channels:

```sql
WITH channel_summary AS (
  SELECT 
    c.channel,
    SUM(ads.spend_inr) AS total_spend,
    COUNT(DISTINCT conv.conversion_id) AS total_orders,
    SUM(conv.revenue_inr) AS attributed_revenue
  FROM campaigns c
  JOIN ad_spend ads ON ads.campaign_id = c.campaign_id
  LEFT JOIN conversions conv ON conv.campaign_id = c.campaign_id
  GROUP BY c.channel
)
SELECT 
  channel,
  ROUND(total_spend, 0) AS spend_inr,
  total_orders,
  ROUND(total_spend / NULLIF(total_orders, 0), 1) AS cac_inr,
  ROUND(attributed_revenue, 0) AS revenue_inr,
  ROUND(attributed_revenue / NULLIF(total_spend, 0), 2) AS roas
FROM channel_summary
ORDER BY roas DESC;
```

**Key Findings Across Marketing Channels:**
- **Email Marketing**: Spend = Rs 4.2 Lakhs, Attributed Orders = 2,958, **CAC = Rs 142**, **ROAS = 4.82x** (Highest ROI channel).
- **Google Search**: Spend = Rs 28.5 Lakhs, Attributed Orders = 10,000, **CAC = Rs 285**, **ROAS = 3.64x** (High intent, stable).
- **Meta Ads**: Spend = Rs 34.0 Lakhs, Attributed Orders = 10,000, **CAC = Rs 340**, **ROAS = 2.41x** (Solid middle-of-funnel scale).
- **Influencer Partnerships**: Spend = Rs 18.0 Lakhs, Attributed Orders = 2,903, **CAC = Rs 620**, **ROAS = 1.22x** (Break-even).
- **YouTube Ads**: Spend = Rs 22.0 Lakhs, Attributed Orders = 2,973, **CAC = Rs 740**, **ROAS = 0.91x** (Unprofitable direct return).

### Core DAX Measures

```dax
-- Return on Ad Spend (ROAS)
ROAS = 
DIVIDE (
    SUM ( Conversions[revenue_inr] ),
    SUM ( AdSpend[spend_inr] ),
    0
)

-- Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
CAC_INR = 
DIVIDE (
    SUM ( AdSpend[spend_inr] ),
    DISTINCTCOUNT ( Conversions[conversion_id] ),
    0
)

-- Relative Conversion Lift %
ConversionLiftPct = 
VAR ControlCR = 
    CALCULATE (
        DIVIDE ( SUM ( ABTest[converted] ), COUNTROWS ( ABTest ), 0 ),
        ABTest[variant] = "control"
    )
VAR TreatmentCR = 
    CALCULATE (
        DIVIDE ( SUM ( ABTest[converted] ), COUNTROWS ( ABTest ), 0 ),
        ABTest[variant] = "treatment"
    )
RETURN 
DIVIDE ( TreatmentCR - ControlCR, ControlCR, 0 ) * 100
```

## 10 likely interview questions & model answers

### 1. Why is checking for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM) essential before analyzing A/B test results?
If a technical defect causes traffic to be routed asymmetrically (e.g. 54/46 instead of 50/50), the experimental groups are no longer random. Confounding variables such as caching anomalies, device-specific redirect drops, or cookie deletion will invalidate the test. Verifying SRM via a chi-square goodness-of-fit test ensures that the experimental sample is uncompromised.

### 2. What does a p-value of 0.0459 mean in business terms?
A p-value of 0.0459 means there is only a 4.59% probability that the observed +1.15 percentage point conversion lift was caused by random sampling noise rather than the free-shipping banner itself. Because this is below the standard $\alpha = 0.05$ significance threshold, we can confidently reject the null hypothesis and recommend a full production rollout.

### 3. What is the difference between statistical significance and practical business significance?
Statistical significance answers whether an observed effect is real rather than noise. Practical business significance answers whether the effect size justifies the engineering and commercial costs of implementation. In this test, a +1.15% conversion lift generates Rs 25.6 Lakhs in incremental gross sales for a banner that costs zero marginal rupees to keep in production, representing clear practical significance.

### 4. Why did you use a two-proportion z-test instead of a t-test?
Conversion is a binary Bernoulli outcome (either a user converts or does not). For large sample sizes (over 6,000 visitors per arm), the binomial distribution converges to the normal distribution by the Central Limit Theorem, making the two-proportion z-test the exact mathematical standard. For continuous metrics like revenue per user, Welch's t-test or bootstrap tests are used.

### 5. Why was YouTube advertising delivering a negative direct ROAS (0.91x)?
YouTube is predominantly a top-of-funnel brand awareness medium. Users watching videos are not in an immediate shopping mindset; clicking an ad disrupts their viewing experience. Without robust multi-touch attribution or immediate remarketing, pure last-click attribution will show poor direct conversion efficiency.

### 6. What is Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) and how did you compute it?
CAC is the total marketing ad spend divided by the number of new acquired customers (`Total Spend / Total Attributed Orders`). Email had a CAC of Rs 142, Google Search Rs 285, and YouTube Rs 740.

### 7. How did you structure your DAX measures in Power BI?
I wrote measures for `ROAS`, `CAC_INR`, and `ConversionLiftPct`. In `ConversionLiftPct`, I used variables (`VAR`) to calculate Control CR and Treatment CR separately using `CALCULATE()` and `DIVIDE()`, returning the relative percentage difference.

### 8. What is the difference between first-touch and last-touch attribution?
First-touch attribution assigns 100% of revenue credit to the initial channel that introduced the user to the brand. Last-touch attribution gives 100% credit to the final channel clicked immediately before purchase. High-intent channels like Brand Search and Email look dominant in last-touch, while discovery channels look better in first-touch.

### 9. What would you do if management insisted on continuing YouTube video campaigns?
I would propose restructuring the campaign format: transition away from broad awareness video campaigns toward YouTube Shorts Direct-Response ads featuring clear promo codes, with strict creative refresh schedules and a 2.0x target ROAS hurdle.

### 10. If the p-value had been 0.08, what would you have recommended?
At $p = 0.08$, the result is not statistically significant at the 95% confidence level. I would check if the experiment was underpowered and whether running it for an additional week to collect more sample would reach significance. If the effect size remained modest, I would recommend deprecating the banner rather than rolling out an unproven change.
