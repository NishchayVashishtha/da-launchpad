## Learning objectives

- Spot "what share of the total" questions.
- Add a grand total next to every row with a window function.
- Compute shares inside each group (for example, each store in its region).
- Rank by value, add a running total, and apply the 80/20 rule.
- Avoid the divide-by-integer trap.

## Why this matters for a Data Analyst job

<RealWorld>
"UPI is 41 percent of our payments." "Five stores make half our regional sales." Managers think in shares, not raw numbers, because a share tells them where to focus. Contribution analysis is behind almost every summary slide, and Pareto (the 80/20 rule) is how you decide which few customers, products or merchants matter most.
</RealWorld>

## Concept

### The recognition cue

- "percent of total", "share of sales", "contribution", "mix"
- "what percentage does each category make up"
- "top customers that make up 80 percent of revenue"
- "cumulative percentage", "Pareto"

### The big idea

You need two numbers on the same row: the row's value, and the total. The old way used a subquery or a self-join to get the total. The modern way is a window function that puts the total next to every row, without collapsing the rows.

<FormulaCard title="Percent of total">
```sql
SELECT category,
       SUM(amount) AS sales,
       ROUND(100.0 * SUM(amount) / SUM(SUM(amount)) OVER (), 1) AS pct_of_total
FROM sales
GROUP BY category;
```
`SUM(SUM(amount)) OVER ()` reads as: first sum per group, then add those group sums across all groups.
</FormulaCard>

### Percent within a group

Add `PARTITION BY` to restart the total for each group:

```sql
SUM(SUM(amount)) OVER (PARTITION BY region)
```

Now every store is compared with the total of its own region, and the shares inside each region add up to 100.

### Cumulative share and Pareto

1. Total per item (customer, merchant, product).
2. Sort descending by value.
3. A running `SUM` in that order gives the cumulative value.
4. Divide by the grand total to get the cumulative share.
5. Keep rows up to the point where the share reaches 80 percent.

```sql
SUM(amt) OVER (ORDER BY amt DESC, id)      -- running total, biggest first
SUM(amt) OVER ()                           -- grand total
```

### Variations

1. **Share within a group:** `PARTITION BY`.
2. **Share of a filtered total:** compute the denominator from only some rows with a conditional `SUM(CASE WHEN ... END) OVER ()`.
3. **Cumulative percent and cut-off** (Pareto).
4. **Month share of the year:** `PARTITION BY year`.
5. **Share growth:** compare shares across two periods.

### Edge cases

- **Integer division:** `100 * a / b` with integers can give 0. Use `100.0 *`.
- **Zero or NULL total:** the division fails or returns NULL. Guard with `NULLIF(total, 0)`.
- **NULL values:** `SUM` ignores NULLs, so rows with NULL amount count as zero. Decide if that is right.
- **Rounding:** shares may add to 99.9 or 100.1. That is normal. Round only at the end.
- **Ties in the running total:** use a tie-breaker in the window's `ORDER BY`, otherwise equal values give the same running total.
- **Pareto boundary:** include the row that crosses 80 percent. Say which rule you used.

<Tip>
The window function runs after `GROUP BY`. That is why you can wrap a normal aggregate inside it: `SUM(SUM(x)) OVER ()`.
</Tip>

## Worked examples

### Example 1: payment mix

How is the money split across payment methods? Only successful payments count.

<SqlPlayground dataset="ecommerce" query={`SELECT payment_method,
       ROUND(SUM(amount), 2) AS total_amount,
       ROUND(100.0 * SUM(amount) / SUM(SUM(amount)) OVER (), 1) AS pct_of_total
FROM payments
WHERE payment_status = 'Success'
GROUP BY payment_method
ORDER BY total_amount DESC, payment_method;`} title="Payment method share" />

Output:

| payment_method | total_amount | pct_of_total |
|---|---|---|
| UPI | 20527685.03 | 41.1 |
| COD | 10029836.81 | 20.1 |
| Credit Card | 7718240.76 | 15.5 |
| Debit Card | 5948798.18 | 11.9 |
| Netbanking | 2932311.75 | 5.9 |
| Wallet | 2745035.72 | 5.5 |


UPI is the largest share and COD is second. The percentages add up to about 100.

### Example 2: share within a region

Each store's contribution to its own region. Exact duplicate sale rows are removed first.

<SqlPlayground dataset="retail" query={`WITH s AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT sale_id, store_id, net_amount_inr
  FROM sales
)
SELECT st.region,
       st.store_name,
       ROUND(SUM(s.net_amount_inr), 0) AS net_sales,
       ROUND(100.0 * SUM(s.net_amount_inr)
             / SUM(SUM(s.net_amount_inr)) OVER (PARTITION BY st.region), 1) AS pct_of_region
FROM s
JOIN stores st ON st.store_id = s.store_id
GROUP BY st.region, st.store_name
ORDER BY st.region, net_sales DESC, st.store_name
LIMIT 8;`} title="Store share of its region" />

Output:

| region | store_name | net_sales | pct_of_region |
|---|---|---|---|
| East | Vasudha Supermarket - Six Mile | 1374128 | 40.4 |
| East | Vasudha Supermarket - Salt Lake | 1310863 | 38.5 |
| East | Vasudha Express - Boring Road | 716319 | 21.1 |
| North | Vasudha Hypermarket - Dwarka | 2431026 | 40.6 |
| North | Vasudha Supermarket - Malviya Nagar | 1360203 | 22.7 |
| North | Vasudha Supermarket - Gomti Nagar | 805132 | 13.4 |
| North | Vasudha Express - Sector 17 | 736147 | 12.3 |
| North | Vasudha Express - Rohini | 656900 | 11 |


The East region has only three stores, so each store's share is large. Inside each region the shares add up to 100. A store with 40 percent of a small region is not necessarily bigger than a store with 15 percent of a big region, so be careful when you compare across regions.

### Example 3: cumulative share of merchants

<SqlPlayground dataset="fintech" query={`WITH t AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT txn_id, merchant_id, amount
  FROM transactions
  WHERE status = 'SUCCESS' AND merchant_id IS NOT NULL
),
m AS (
  SELECT merchant_id, SUM(amount) AS amt
  FROM t
  GROUP BY merchant_id
)
SELECT ROW_NUMBER() OVER (ORDER BY amt DESC, merchant_id) AS rank_no,
       merchant_id,
       ROUND(amt, 0) AS amount,
       ROUND(100.0 * SUM(amt) OVER (ORDER BY amt DESC, merchant_id)
             / SUM(amt) OVER (), 1) AS cum_pct
FROM m
ORDER BY rank_no
LIMIT 8;`} title="Cumulative share, biggest first" />

Output:

| rank_no | merchant_id | amount | cum_pct |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 339 | 2291040 | 9.7 |
| 2 | 397 | 1180645 | 14.7 |
| 3 | 132 | 993611 | 18.9 |
| 4 | 12 | 982407 | 23.1 |
| 5 | 57 | 733451 | 26.2 |
| 6 | 103 | 678953 | 29.1 |
| 7 | 289 | 647168 | 31.8 |
| 8 | 239 | 572342 | 34.3 |


Each row adds one more merchant, and `cum_pct` shows how much of the total the top merchants cover together. The first few merchants already hold a noticeable share.

### Example 4: how many merchants make 80 percent?

<SqlPlayground dataset="fintech" query={`WITH t AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT txn_id, merchant_id, amount
  FROM transactions
  WHERE status = 'SUCCESS' AND merchant_id IS NOT NULL
),
m AS (
  SELECT merchant_id, SUM(amount) AS amt FROM t GROUP BY merchant_id
),
c AS (
  SELECT amt,
         SUM(amt) OVER (ORDER BY amt DESC, merchant_id) AS running,
         SUM(amt) OVER () AS total,
         ROW_NUMBER() OVER (ORDER BY amt DESC, merchant_id) AS rk
  FROM m
)
SELECT MIN(rk) AS merchants_for_80pct,
       (SELECT COUNT(*) FROM m) AS total_merchants,
       ROUND(100.0 * MIN(rk) / (SELECT COUNT(*) FROM m), 1) AS pct_of_merchants
FROM c
WHERE running >= 0.8 * total;`} title="Pareto cut-off" />

Output:

| merchants_for_80pct | total_merchants | pct_of_merchants |
|---|---|---|
| 104 | 400 | 26.0 |


About a quarter of merchants produce 80 percent of value. That is close to, but not exactly, the "80/20" rule of thumb. The real number is something you measure, not assume.

## Common mistakes

<CommonMistake>
Writing `100 * SUM(x) / SUM(x) OVER ()` without grouping, or `100 * a / b` with integers. You either get an error or a result of 0 for every row. Use `100.0 *` and group first.
</CommonMistake>

<CommonMistake>
Using the grand total when the question asks for the share within a group. Without `PARTITION BY` the shares add up to 100 across the whole table, not within each group.
</CommonMistake>

<CommonMistake>
Filtering rows in the same `SELECT` that computes the window total. The `WHERE` runs first, so the total only covers the filtered rows, and the top row shows 100 percent. Compute the shares in a CTE, then filter in the outer query.
</CommonMistake>

<CommonMistake>
Taking "top 20 percent of customers" by count and assuming they make 80 percent of revenue. Measure the real cut-off with a cumulative share.
</CommonMistake>

<CommonMistake>
Running total with no tie-breaker. Equal values receive the same running total, and the cut-off row becomes ambiguous.
</CommonMistake>

## Interview angle

<InterviewAlert>
Share questions look easy, but interviewers check the denominator. Always say "share of what?" (grand total, region, month) before you write the query.
</InterviewAlert>

<InterviewQ q="How do you calculate each product's percentage of total sales?">
Group by product, then divide each product's sum by the grand total using a window: `100.0 * SUM(sales) / SUM(SUM(sales)) OVER ()`. I use `100.0` to avoid integer division and `ROUND` at the end. If I need a share within a category I add `PARTITION BY category`.
</InterviewQ>

<InterviewQ q="How do you find the customers who generate 80 percent of revenue?">
I total revenue per customer, sort descending, take a running `SUM` with `OVER (ORDER BY revenue DESC, customer_id)`, and divide by the grand total from `SUM(...) OVER ()`. I keep rows until the cumulative share first reaches 80 percent, including the row that crosses it.
</InterviewQ>

<InterviewQ q="What could go wrong with a percent of total query?">
Integer division giving 0, a zero or NULL total, a filter that changes the denominator, duplicates inflating the numerator, and unclear definitions of the total. I guard with `100.0`, `NULLIF`, a CTE for filtering, and I confirm what the total means.
</InterviewQ>

<InterviewQ q="What does the Pareto principle tell a business?">
That a small share of customers, products or merchants usually produces most of the value, so effort should be focused there. The exact split must be measured; it may be 70/30 or 90/10, not always 80/20.
</InterviewQ>

## Key takeaways

- Percent of total = row value divided by a window total on the same row.
- Use `SUM(SUM(x)) OVER ()` after grouping, and add `PARTITION BY` for within-group shares.
- Use `100.0` and guard zero totals with `NULLIF`.
- Pareto = value per item, sorted descending, running SUM, divide by total, find the 80 percent cut.
- Say what the denominator is before you write the query.
