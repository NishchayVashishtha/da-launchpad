## Why this matters for a Data Analyst job

<RealWorld>
Many Indian companies screen freshers with a 30 to 45 minute Excel test: a workbook with raw data and 8 to 12 tasks, sent by email or done on a shared laptop. Services firms, MIS teams in banks and e-commerce operations teams use this a lot. Candidates fail not because the tasks are hard, but because they are slow, skip checks or miss a small instruction. This lesson is your rehearsal.
</RealWorld>

## Concept

Test tasks fall into a small number of families. If you can do each family fast, you can pass most tests.

| Family | Typical asks | Tools |
|---|---|---|
| Clean up | remove duplicates, trim names, fix dates, split a column | TRIM, Text to Columns, Remove Duplicates, Flash Fill |
| Conditional logic | label rows Late/On time, grade bands | IF, IFS, AND, OR |
| Lookups | find the price for an ID, merge two sheets | XLOOKUP, VLOOKUP, INDEX-MATCH |
| Conditional totals | revenue per city, count of cancelled orders | SUMIFS, COUNTIFS, AVERAGEIFS |
| Dates and text | weekday, month, extract a code | TEXT, MONTH, LEFT, MID, DATEDIF |
| Summaries | pivot, chart, slicer | Pivot table, pivot chart |
| Presentation | format, freeze panes, validation, print area | CF, Data Validation |
| Dynamic arrays | unique list, filter rows, sort | UNIQUE, FILTER, SORT |

**Test-day strategy.**

1. Read all tasks first (one minute). Start with the ones you can finish in two minutes.
2. Convert the data to a Table (Ctrl + T) at the start. Formulas then read like `tbl_food_orders[order_value_inr]`.
3. Never type numbers; use formulas so the answer stays correct if the data changes.
4. Add a row of checks: totals that must match, counts that must add up.
5. Follow the output format exactly: column H for the cities, column I for the totals, if the question says so.
6. Keep a clean look: header bold, number formats, no stray cells.
7. Save every 5 minutes (Ctrl + S).
8. If stuck for more than 3 minutes, move on and return.

<FormulaCard title="Formula patterns to know by heart">
Sum with condition: =SUMIFS(sum_range, criteria_range1, crit1, criteria_range2, crit2)
Count with condition: `=COUNTIFS(range, crit)`
Average with condition: `=AVERAGEIFS(avg_range, range, crit)`
Lookup: `=XLOOKUP(key, key_column, return_column, "Not found")`
Lookup (old): `=INDEX(return_col, MATCH(key, key_col, 0))`
Band labels: `=IFS(x<3,"Short", x<=6,"Medium", TRUE,"Long")`
</FormulaCard>

## Worked examples

Practice with the simulated test. **Time limit: 40 minutes.** Do not read the solutions until you have tried.

<Download href="data/excel/excel-test-practice.xlsx" label="excel-test-practice.xlsx" />

The sheet `food_orders` has 2,000 deliveries. The `instructions` sheet holds the 10 tasks. Below are the tasks with model solutions, checked against the data. Cell references assume you converted the data to the table `tbl_food_orders`.

**Task 1: SUMIFS by city.** Total `order_value_inr` per `restaurant_city`. List cities in H, totals in I.
Solution: in H2 `=SORT(UNIQUE(FILTER(tbl_food_orders[restaurant_city],tbl_food_orders[restaurant_city]<>"")))`, then in I2 `=SUMIFS(tbl_food_orders[order_value_inr],tbl_food_orders[restaurant_city],H2)` filled down. Without dynamic arrays, type the city names.
Expected: Delhi 1,95,684; Mumbai 1,66,570; Bengaluru 1,27,188; Hyderabad 1,03,616; Chennai 79,485; Ahmedabad 68,411; Kolkata 66,912; Pune 60,555; Jaipur 37,895; Lucknow 30,745. Check: 54 rows have a blank city, so these ten do not add up to the grand total of 9,64,024. The difference is the blank-city orders.

**Task 2: COUNTIFS cancelled by cuisine.** `=COUNTIFS(tbl_food_orders[cuisine],A2,tbl_food_orders[delivery_status],"Cancelled")`.
Expected: North Indian 17; South Indian 16; Chinese 8; Biryani 7; Desserts 7; Gujarati Thali 7; Mughlai 6; Burgers 4; Pizza 4; Healthy 3; Street Food 3; Bengali 2. Total 84.

**Task 3: AVERAGEIFS delay by weather (Delivered only).** `=AVERAGEIFS(tbl_food_orders[delay_minutes],tbl_food_orders[weather],A2,tbl_food_orders[delivery_status],"Delivered")`.
Expected: Clear 1.31; Cloudy 1.81; Rain 6.56; Heavy Rain 12.71 minutes. Insight: heavy rain adds about 11 minutes compared with clear weather.

**Task 4: COUNTIF and IF.** `=COUNTIF(tbl_food_orders[delay_minutes],">15")` returns **141**. New column `late_flag`: `=IF([@[delay_minutes]]>15,"Late","On time")`. Careful: cancelled orders have a blank delay, and a blank counts as 0, so they show "On time". Better: `=IF([@[delivery_status]]="Cancelled","",IF([@[delay_minutes]]>15,"Late","On time"))`.

**Task 5: IFS bands.** `=IFS([@[distance_km]]<3,"Short",[@[distance_km]]<=6,"Medium",TRUE,"Long")`.
Expected counts: Short 690, Medium 998, Long 312 (total 2,000).

**Task 6: INDEX and MATCH.** In K2 type `10020`. In L2: `=INDEX(tbl_food_orders[order_value_inr],MATCH(K2,tbl_food_orders[delivery_id],0))` returns **519**. The `0` means exact match. Mention XLOOKUP as the modern option: `=XLOOKUP(K2,tbl_food_orders[delivery_id],tbl_food_orders[order_value_inr])`.

**Task 7: UNIQUE then SORT.** `=SORT(UNIQUE(tbl_food_orders[cuisine]))` returns 12 cuisines from Bengali to Street Food.

**Task 8: FILTER with two conditions.** `=FILTER(tbl_food_orders,(tbl_food_orders[delivery_status]="Cancelled")*(tbl_food_orders[weather]="Heavy Rain"),"None")`. Expected: **9** rows. Multiplication acts as AND; addition acts as OR.

**Task 9: weekday names.** New column `weekday_name`: `=TEXT([@[order_date]],"dddd")`. Count with `=COUNTIF(tbl_food_orders[weekday_name],A2)`.
Expected: Sunday 341, Saturday 340, Thursday 280, Tuesday 277, Monday 255, Wednesday 254, Friday 253. Weekends are the busiest.

**Task 10: on-time rate per city.** On-time means `delay_minutes <= 0` among Delivered orders. `=COUNTIFS(city_col,H2,status_col,"Delivered",delay_col,"<=0")/COUNTIFS(city_col,H2,status_col,"Delivered")`.
Expected: Lucknow 57.5% (42/73); Hyderabad 49.8% (109/219); Pune 47.0% (62/132); Chennai 46.1% (76/165); Delhi 45.8% (162/354); Mumbai 45.1% (153/339); Jaipur 44.3% (35/79); Ahmedabad 39.4% (37/94); Bengaluru 39.0% (103/264); Kolkata 38.5% (55/143).

**Scoring yourself.** 1 point per correct task, plus 0.5 for each of: Table used, formulas not hard-coded, clean formatting. Under 8: repeat in two days. Over 11 of 15 within 40 minutes: you are test-ready.

<Warning>
Read the unit and the filter in every task. "Delivered orders only" and "all orders" give different answers, and tests are written to catch this.
</Warning>

## Common mistakes

<CommonMistake>Hard-coding answers (typing 141 instead of a COUNTIF). Many tests check formulas, not only values.</CommonMistake>

<CommonMistake>Forgetting absolute references when filling down (`$A$2` vs `A2`), so ranges slide and totals are wrong. Tables avoid this.</CommonMistake>

<CommonMistake>Using approximate match in VLOOKUP by leaving out the last argument. Use FALSE or 0 for exact match.</CommonMistake>

<CommonMistake>Spending 20 minutes on a hard task and leaving easy tasks blank. Do the quick wins first.</CommonMistake>

<CommonMistake>Ignoring blanks. Blank delay values for cancelled orders can look like zero and spoil an average or a flag.</CommonMistake>

<CommonMistake>Not checking against a total. Always compare a sum of parts with the grand total.</CommonMistake>

## Interview angle

<InterviewAlert>
After the test, the interviewer often asks "walk me through how you solved Task 4". Be ready to explain every formula in plain words and say what you checked.
</InterviewAlert>

<InterviewQ q="How do you prepare for an Excel test in two days?">
Day one: practise the formula families (SUMIFS, COUNTIFS, IFS, XLOOKUP and INDEX-MATCH, text and date functions) and pivot tables on a sample dataset. Day two: do one timed mock like the 10-task set, review mistakes, and rehearse shortcuts. On the day: convert to a Table, read all tasks first, do easy ones first, and keep checks.
</InterviewQ>

<InterviewQ q="VLOOKUP vs XLOOKUP vs INDEX-MATCH?">
VLOOKUP looks only to the right and breaks when columns are inserted if you use a column number. INDEX-MATCH can look in any direction and is stable. XLOOKUP does the same with simpler syntax, a built-in "not found" message and exact match by default, but needs Excel 2021 or 365. I use XLOOKUP when available and INDEX-MATCH when I must support older versions.
</InterviewQ>

<InterviewQ q="How do you verify that your Excel answer is right?">
I cross-check with a second method: a pivot against SUMIFS, sum of parts against the grand total, count of rows before and after filtering, and a spot check of two or three rows by hand.
</InterviewQ>

## Key takeaways

- Tests repeat the same task families; master each family.
- Use a Table, formulas (not typed numbers) and exact output format.
- Read every filter: "Delivered only" changes the answer.
- Sanity-check with totals and a second method.
- Work easy tasks first; use shortcuts; save often.
