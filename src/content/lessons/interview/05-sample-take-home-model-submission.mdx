## Learning objectives

- Translate an ambiguous business brief into four structured technical tasks and deliverables.
- Perform baseline data hygiene checks in SQLite to handle duplicate rows, missing values, and outliers.
- Query transaction volumes and failure rates across partner banks, client applications, and time windows.
- Draft an executive-ready one-page memo containing headline findings, root causes, and quantified recommendations.
- Benchmark candidate work against an objective 5-part evaluation rubric used by hiring teams.

## Why this matters for a Data Analyst job

The fastest way to master take-home assignments is to study a complete, production-grade model submission. Most candidates understand SQL syntax in isolation, but struggle to connect the dots: How do you document assumptions? How do you choose between presenting a raw table or a summary? How do technical failure codes translate into business recommendations that an Operations Director will fund?

This lesson presents a real-world scenario from a fictional digital payments platform ("PayZippy"). You will review the original task brief, study the exact queries and outputs, and read the final one-page executive summary that secured an interview.

<RealWorld>
In a fast-growing Indian payments company processing over ₹50 Crore in monthly UPI volume, a 0.5% increase in failure rate results in thousands of angry merchants and over ₹15 Lakh in lost checkout commissions. When leadership requests a take-home analysis, they are not grading academic code elegance; they are searching for an analyst who can pinpoint the exact failing infrastructure component, calculate excess failure costs, and deliver a risk-managed rollout plan.
</RealWorld>

## Concept

### The business brief

Imagine receiving the following email from the Head of Operations at digital payments platform PayZippy:

> **Take-Home Brief: UPI Transaction Failure Investigation**
> 
> *Hi Candidate,*
> 
> *Over the past several quarters, our merchant partners have raised concerns regarding elevated UPI transaction failures. Our engineering logs are available in the attached `fintech` database (`transactions` and `failed_transactions` tables).*
> 
> *Please complete the following in 4 hours:*
> 1. *Calculate our overall failure rate and evaluate the monthly failure trend over the last 24 months.*
> 2. *Identify where failures concentrate: isolate specific partner banks, client payment apps, time-of-day windows, and transaction amount bands.*
> 3. *Analyze our primary failure codes and assess whether customer retries effectively recover failed transactions.*
> 4. *Provide three concrete, data-backed operational recommendations with projected recovery impact.*
> 
> *Deliverable: A clean SQL script and a 1-page executive summary.*

### The hiring manager grading rubric

Submissions are evaluated against five weighted criteria (total 100 points):

| Criteria | Weight | What Earns Top Marks (4/4) | What Triggers Deductions (1-2/4) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Data Hygiene & Assumptions** | 25% | Identifies 89 duplicate rows; excludes PENDING cleanly; notes P2P null merchants. | Aggregates raw table with duplicates; silent assumptions on PENDING. |
| **Analytical Rigor** | 20% | Correct failure rate formulas; isolates Kaveri Bank and NammaPay outage windows. | Confuses transaction counts with failure rates; ignores denominator scale. |
| **Root Cause Depth** | 20% | Pinpoints Kaveri Bank evening server timeouts and NammaPay discrete outage dates. | Generic claims like 'users have low balance' without segmenting. |
| **Executive Communication** | 20% | Crisp 1-page memo leading with quantified impact; clean tables; no jargon. | 15-page dump of raw code; burying findings under process descriptions. |
| **Actionable Recommendations** | 15% | Proposes error-code specific retry policies with empirical recovery rates. | Vague advice like 'improve server performance' or 'talk to banks'. |

## Worked examples

### Step 1: Initial data hygiene and assumption verification

We first inspect total records versus unique transaction IDs:

<SqlPlayground
  dataset="fintech"
  query={`SELECT
  COUNT(*) AS total_rows,
  COUNT(DISTINCT txn_id) AS unique_txn_ids
FROM transactions;`}
  title="Step 1: Duplicate Check on Transactions"
/>

**Finding:** The table contains **22,389 rows** but only **22,300 unique transactions** (89 exact duplicates). We establish an explicit analytical rule: all subsequent queries will deduplicate by `txn_id` using a CTE `clean`, and failure rate will be defined strictly as `FAILED / (SUCCESS + FAILED)`.

### Step 2: Monthly failure rate trend

Next, we evaluate whether failures are steady or characterized by sudden spikes:

<SqlPlayground
  dataset="fintech"
  query={`WITH clean AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT txn_id, status, strftime('%Y-%m', txn_datetime) AS month
  FROM transactions
)
SELECT
  month,
  COUNT(*) AS n_txns,
  ROUND(100.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN status = 'FAILED' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) / COUNT(*), 2) AS fail_pct
FROM clean
GROUP BY month
ORDER BY month ASC
LIMIT 12;`}
  title="Step 2: Monthly Failure Trend (First 12 Months)"
/>

**Finding:** Baseline failure rate normally fluctuates between **1.5% and 2.2%**. However, four distinct months exhibit dramatic spikes: March 2023 (7.12%), September 2023 (6.28%), February 2024 (4.99%), and July 2024 (4.46%).

### Step 3: Failure concentration by bank and client app

We segment failure rates by partner bank to calculate excess failure volume:

<SqlPlayground
  dataset="fintech"
  query={`WITH clean AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT txn_id, bank, status
  FROM transactions
),
overall AS (
  SELECT 1.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN status = 'FAILED' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) / COUNT(*) AS overall_rate
  FROM clean
),
bank_stats AS (
  SELECT
    bank,
    SUM(CASE WHEN status = 'FAILED' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS failed,
    COUNT(*) AS n_txns
  FROM clean
  GROUP BY bank
)
SELECT
  bank,
  failed,
  ROUND(n_txns * (SELECT overall_rate FROM overall), 2) AS expected,
  ROUND(failed - n_txns * (SELECT overall_rate FROM overall), 2) AS excess
FROM bank_stats
ORDER BY excess DESC, bank ASC
LIMIT 3;`}
  title="Step 3: Excess Failures by Partner Bank"
/>

**Finding:** **Kaveri Co-op Bank** generated **103 failures** compared to an expected 45.82 at network average, contributing **57.18 excess failures**. It has a **6.06% failure rate**, while all other partner banks maintain rates between 2.16% and 2.94%.

### Step 4: Hourly bottlenecks and ticket size analysis

We cross-tabulate Kaveri Bank's performance by hour of day:

<SqlPlayground
  dataset="fintech"
  query={`WITH clean AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT txn_id, bank, status, strftime('%H', txn_datetime) AS hour
  FROM transactions
  WHERE bank = 'Kaveri Co-op Bank'
)
SELECT
  hour,
  COUNT(*) AS n_txns,
  ROUND(100.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN status = 'FAILED' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) / COUNT(*), 2) AS fail_pct
FROM clean
GROUP BY hour
ORDER BY fail_pct DESC, hour ASC
LIMIT 5;`}
  title="Step 4: Kaveri Co-op Bank Hourly Failure Peak"
/>

**Finding:** Excluding the tiny 2-transaction sample at hour 04, Kaveri Bank's failures cluster severely during peak evening retail hours: **hour 20 (12.98%)**, **hour 09 (11.65%)**, and **hour 19 (9.88%)**. Core banking systems become congested during evening rush hours.

### Step 5: Failure code Pareto and retry recovery efficiency

We evaluate which failure codes recover when customers or systems trigger retries:

<SqlPlayground
  dataset="fintech"
  query={`WITH clean_failed AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT failed_txn_id, failure_code, retried, retry_succeeded
  FROM failed_transactions
  WHERE retried = 1
)
SELECT
  failure_code,
  COUNT(*) AS retried_n,
  SUM(CASE WHEN retry_succeeded = 1 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS recovered_n,
  ROUND(100.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN retry_succeeded = 1 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) / COUNT(*), 2) AS recovery_pct
FROM clean_failed
GROUP BY failure_code
ORDER BY recovery_pct DESC, retried_n DESC, failure_code ASC;`}
  title="Step 5: Retry Recovery Success Rate by Error Code"
/>

**Finding:** Technical timeout errors recover remarkably well: **`BANK_SERVER_TIMEOUT` recovers at 74.07%** and `NETWORK_ERROR` recovers at 67.35%. In contrast, user balance issues (`INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`) recover only **13.46%** of the time.

### Step 6: The final 1-page executive submission

Below is the complete, formatted executive summary submitted to the hiring manager:

> ### Executive Memorandum: UPI Transaction Failure Diagnosis & Action Plan
> **To:** Head of Operations, PayZippy  
> **From:** Data Analyst Candidate  
> **Date:** November 2024  
> **Objective:** Diagnose elevated payment failure rates across 22,300 unique transactions and propose operational remediation.
> 
> ---
> 
> #### 1. Key Findings & Root Cause Analysis
> - **Overall System Performance:** The baseline UPI failure rate across the network is **2.70%** (601 failed transactions out of 22,300 deduplicated records). However, failures are not uniformly distributed.
> - **Primary Infrastructure Bottleneck (Kaveri Co-op Bank):** Kaveri Co-op Bank exhibits a **6.06% failure rate**, generating **57.18 excess failures** (nearly 25% of all network failures despite processing only 7.6% of volume). Failures peak during commercial rush hours (9:00 AM and 7:00–8:00 PM) at **11.6% to 13.0%**, predominantly logged as `BANK_SERVER_TIMEOUT`.
> - **Client App Outage Clusters (NammaPay):** The NammaPay payment app averaged a **5.10% failure rate**. Investigation isolates this entirely to four discrete outage days (2023-03-14, 2023-09-21, 2024-02-06, and 2024-07-16) where failure rates exceeded **31% to 40%** due to `UPI_SYSTEM_DOWN`. On normal days, NammaPay operates at a healthy 2.0% baseline.
> - **High-Value Limit Bottlenecks:** Transactions exceeding ₹50,000 fail at **10.0%** (vs 2.66% for micro-transactions), driven by user banking transfer caps (`LIMIT_EXCEEDED`).
> 
> #### 2. Operational Recommendations & Projected Impact
> 1. **Implement Automated Smart Retries for Server Timeouts:** Deploy an in-app automated retry with exponential backoff (2s, 5s) exclusively for `BANK_SERVER_TIMEOUT` and `NETWORK_ERROR` codes. Retries for timeout errors currently exhibit a **74.07% recovery rate**. This will recover ~30 failed transactions monthly, reducing Kaveri Bank's net failure rate to ~3.5%.
> 2. **Dynamic In-App Bank Health Banners:** When Kaveri Bank failure rates exceed 8% during evening peak hours, trigger a gentle UI notification advising users to switch to an alternative linked bank account.
> 3. **Proactive Outage Circuit-Breakers for NammaPay:** Establish real-time telemetry alerting operations when NammaPay failure rates exceed 10% in a 15-minute rolling window, automatically routing transactions to secondary gateway bridges.
> 
> #### 3. Data Assumptions & Limitations
> - Deduplicated 89 duplicate `txn_id` records. Excluded 89 PENDING transactions from failure rate calculations.
> - Analysis does not account for user lifetime value (LTV) or merchant churn costs. Recommended next step is to link transaction logs to 30-day merchant retention data.

## Common mistakes

<CommonMistake>
Reporting failure rate using the uncleaned raw row count (22,389), which includes 89 duplicate records and produces conflicting numbers with the evaluator's answer key.
</CommonMistake>

<CommonMistake>
Treating PENDING transactions (89 rows) as successful payments, which artificially deflates the reported failure rate.
</CommonMistake>

<CommonMistake>
Over-indexing on hour 04's 50% failure rate for Kaveri Bank without noticing that it represents only 2 total transactions (1 failure, 1 success).
</CommonMistake>

<CommonMistake>
Recommending blind automatic retries for all transactions, ignoring that retrying INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS succeeds only 13% of the time and creates unnecessary load on banking servers.
</CommonMistake>

<CommonMistake>
Writing a 15-page essay describing your SQL syntax line by line instead of packaging findings into an executive-ready memo with bold headings and bullet points.
</CommonMistake>

## Interview angle

<InterviewAlert>
When discussing a completed take-home assignment, interviewers will challenge your metric definitions. Be prepared to defend why you excluded PENDING transactions and how you isolated outliers.
</InterviewAlert>

<InterviewQ q="Walk me through how you structured your take-home analysis on UPI payment failures.">
I structured my analysis into four distinct phases. First, I verified data hygiene, identifying and deduplicating 89 duplicate rows to ensure clean counts. Second, I examined the 24-month trend, discovering that failures were not random but clustered in specific months and hours. Third, I segmented failures across banks and apps, isolating that Kaveri Co-op Bank accounted for 57 excess failures due to evening peak-hour timeouts, while NammaPay suffered four discrete outage days. Finally, I synthesized the findings into an executive memo proposing smart retries for timeout codes, which currently recover 74% of failed transactions.
</InterviewQ>

<InterviewQ q="How did you decide whether to include PENDING transactions in your failure rate denominator?">
I treated PENDING transactions as unconfirmed states rather than terminal outcomes. Including them as successes would artificially deflate the failure rate, while counting them as failures would overstate technical errors. Therefore, I explicitly documented the decision in my assumptions log: failure rate was strictly calculated over resolved transactions as `FAILED / (SUCCESS + FAILED)`. I also verified that PENDING records accounted for only 0.4% of total volume, ensuring that excluding them did not distort baseline benchmarks.
</InterviewQ>

<InterviewQ q="What would be your immediate next step if this recommendation was approved by leadership?">
My immediate next step would be setting up an A/B test for the automated retry policy. We would roll out the exponential backoff retry mechanism to 10% of users experiencing `BANK_SERVER_TIMEOUT` errors with Kaveri Co-op Bank. I would monitor two metrics daily: the primary metric (recovered payment percentage) and the guardrail metric (average checkout latency). Once we confirm that checkout latency remains within acceptable thresholds, we would scale the policy to 100% of partner banks.
</InterviewQ>

## Key takeaways

- Study high-scoring submissions to understand how empirical SQL outputs transform into persuasive business prose.
- Always lead with an executive memo: state overall failure volume, root cause concentration, and quantified fixes upfront.
- Calculate excess failures to demonstrate commercial prioritization rather than relying solely on raw percentages.
- Differentiate between chronic infrastructure bottlenecks (Kaveri Bank peak hours) and acute outage events (NammaPay discrete dates).
- Propose error-specific product solutions: retries work exceptionally well for timeouts (74%), but fail for insufficient funds (13%).
