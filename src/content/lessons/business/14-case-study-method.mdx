## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Structure any analytics case study prompt into 6 systematic steps: Clarify, Structure, Inspect, Analyze, Synthesize, and Recommend.
- Avoid premature data querying by constructing a MECE hypothesis tree.
- Format written and verbal case responses using the 1-Line Answer + 3 Key Findings + Recommendation structure.
- Self-evaluate case solutions using an objective 100-point rubric across Structure, Analysis, Insight, and Recommendation.
- Navigate our curated repository of 25 industry case studies to build real-world problem-solving fluency.

## Why this matters for a Data Analyst job

<RealWorld>
The case study round is the ultimate filter in senior and entry-level data analyst hiring across top tech companies, product startups, and analytics consultancies. Interviewers give you a messy prompt: *"Our payment failure rate surged 30% yesterday"* or *"DesiCart sales plummeted in December. Tell us why and how to fix it."*

Candidates who fail usually open SQL immediately and start writing random `GROUP BY` queries without a clear plan. They find isolated numbers, get lost in details, and run out of time. Candidates who pass act like seasoned consultants: they pause, ask clarifying questions, build a structured hypothesis tree, run targeted queries to validate or invalidate each branch, and present an executive recommendation backed by numbers.
</RealWorld>

Solving business cases is not about luck; it is a learned, repeatable engineering discipline. Once you master the 6-step framework, no ambiguous business challenge will intimidate you.

## Concept

### The 6-step case-solving framework

Follow this 6-step workflow for every live case round, take-home challenge, and executive problem:

```text
Step 1: RESTATE & CLARIFY (3-5 minutes)
Paraphrase the problem in your own words. Confirm the North Star metric, scope, time window, and constraints.

Step 2: BUILD A MECE HYPOTHESIS TREE (5 minutes)
Decompose the metric into Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive branches BEFORE looking at data tables.

Step 3: DATA INSPECTION & HYGIENE (5 minutes)
Audit table schemas, check primary keys, deduplicate, trim whitespace, and verify date boundaries.

Step 4: TARGETED ANALYSIS & SEGMENTATION (10-15 minutes)
Query the database systematically to test each hypothesis branch: segment by channel, cohort, bank, or city.

Step 5: SYNTHESIZE KEY INSIGHTS ("SO WHAT?") (3-5 minutes)
Translate statistical findings into operational realities: why did this happen, and what is the business impact?

Step 6: EXECUTIVE RECOMMENDATION & RISKS (3-5 minutes)
Deliver actionable next steps with estimated revenue/cost impact, implementation timelines, and trade-offs.
```

<Tip>
**The MECE Test: Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive**  
When building your hypothesis tree, ensure that:
1. **Mutually Exclusive**: No two branches overlap or test the same underlying factor.
2. **Collectively Exhaustive**: Together, all branches cover every reasonable explanation for the metric movement.
</Tip>

### Structuring the final response

Whether presenting on a whiteboard or writing an executive memo, format your deliverable using this proven structure:

1. **One-Line Executive Answer**: High-level diagnosis and total financial/operational magnitude.
2. **Three Core Diagnostic Findings**: Quantified root-cause drivers with percentages and baseline comparisons.
3. **Actionable Recommendation**:
   - *Immediate Action* (Days 1–7): Quick technical or routing fix.
   - *Medium-Term Strategy* (Weeks 2–6): Product redesign, policy adjustment, or partner SLA enforcement.
4. **Risks and Mitigations**: Honest acknowledgement of trade-offs, potential customer friction, and guardrail metrics.

### Case evaluation rubric (100 points)

Interviewers and hiring managers evaluate solutions across 4 distinct dimensions:

- **Structure & Problem Framing (25 pts)**: Did the candidate ask clarifying questions, define the metric, and construct a logical MECE tree?
- **Analytical Rigor & Data Hygiene (25 pts)**: Did the candidate verify data quality, clean dirty strings, deduplicate webhooks, and calculate accurate numbers?
- **Business Insight & 'So What?' (25 pts)**: Did the candidate move beyond stating observations to explain the underlying business mechanism?
- **Actionable Recommendation & Communication (25 pts)**: Is the proposed solution realistic, prioritized, trade-off-aware, and clearly communicated?

## Worked examples

### 1. End-to-end case walkthrough: Kaveri Bank peak failures

Let's observe the 6-step framework applied to a real fintech case:

```text
Prompt: "Our overall UPI failure rate is 2.70% this month. The engineering team claims all systems are normal. Find out what is happening."

Step 1: Clarify
- Define Failure Rate: Failed Transactions / Total Initiated Attempts.
- Scope: 22,300 total transactions across 8 partner banks.

Step 2: Build MECE Tree
- Branch 1: Platform-wide technical failures (our app/switch).
- Branch 2: Partner bank rail failures (issuing or acquiring bank systems).
- Branch 3: Time-windowed congestion (peak hours vs off-peak).

Step 3 & 4: Inspect & Analyze
- Platform benchmark across 7 partner banks: 2.19% to 2.49% failure rate.
- Kaveri Co-operative Bank: 6.06% overall failure rate across 1,700 transactions.
- Hour-by-hour segmentation: Kaveri Bank failures spike to 9.98% during rush hours (09:00, 18:00, 19:00 IST), while other banks stay stable at 2.19%.

Step 5: Synthesize Insight
The issue is not internal platform instability. Kaveri Bank's Core Banking System (CBS) suffers severe connection pool exhaustion during peak commute hours, driving 68 failed transactions.

Step 6: Recommend
1. Immediate: Implement dynamic routing to throttle Kaveri Bank traffic during peak commute hours and prompt users to select alternative linked accounts.
2. Medium-Term: Issue formal partner SLA breach notice and mandate server connection pool expansion.
```

### 2. Weak answer vs. Strong answer side by side

| Dimension | What a Weak Answer Looks Like | What a Strong Answer Looks Like |
|---|---|---|
| **Approach** | Dives immediately into running queries: *"I ran a query on the orders table and noticed sales were down in December."* | States framework upfront: *"I will investigate this revenue decline by decomposing revenue into Delivered Volume and AOV, then slicing across category and channel."* |
| **Analysis** | Lists isolated observations without context: *"Electronics revenue was Rs 12 lakhs. Fashion returns were high."* | Connects volume and ticket size: *"Delivered revenue contracted 20.5% (Rs 5.32L drop), driven by a 14.7% volume drop combined with a 6.8% AOV compression."* |
| **Recommendation** | Vague platitudes: *"We should improve marketing and provide discounts to increase sales."* | Quantified, phased actions: *"Reallocate Rs 45,000 from low-ROAS influencer spend (0.64x) to Google Search (5.76x) to recover Rs 2.1L in gross sales."* |

## Common mistakes

<CommonMistake>
**Common Mistake: The 'Premature Querying' Trap**  
Candidates frequently jump into SQL within 30 seconds of reading the prompt. Without structuring hypotheses first, they chase random red herrings, write disjointed queries, and fail to synthesize a coherent story when time runs out.
</CommonMistake>

Other frequent pitfalls:
- **Framework vomiting**: Reciting textbook acronyms (SWOT, PESTLE, 4Ps) that have zero relevance to a quantitative data analytics case.
- **Ignoring data hygiene**: Failing to filter out test accounts, uncleaned trailing spaces, or cancelled orders, producing inflated numbers.
- **Leaving out recommendations**: Ending the case with *"So that is why numbers dropped"* without providing actionable business solutions.
- **Overclaiming from tiny sample sizes**: Recommending major product overhauls based on an anomaly involving only 3 or 4 customer records.

## Interview angle

<InterviewAlert>
**How interviewers evaluate case study rounds**  
Interviewers care more about your problem-solving process than reaching a single 'correct' answer. They evaluate whether you remain calm, structure ambiguous prompts, handle real-world dirty data, and deliver clear business recommendations.
</InterviewAlert>

<InterviewQ q="You have 30 minutes to solve an unfamiliar business case. How do you allocate your time?">
I divide a 30-minute case into four distinct time blocks:

1. **Minutes 0–5 (Clarify & Structure)**: Restate the prompt, confirm the core metric, ask clarifying scoping questions, and sketch a 3-branch MECE hypothesis tree.
2. **Minutes 5–15 (Data Hygiene & Targeted Analysis)**: Run quick hygiene checks (duplicates, date windows, string normalization) and execute targeted queries to test each hypothesis branch.
3. **Minutes 15–22 (Synthesize Insights & Impact)**: Identify the primary driver ('So What?'), quantify the financial impact in INR or volume, and determine the operational mechanism.
4. **Minutes 22–30 (Structure Recommendation & Present)**: Formulate a 2-part recommendation (immediate technical fix + medium-term policy), identify operational trade-off risks, and present following the 1-Line Answer + 3 Findings framework.
</InterviewQ>

<InterviewQ q="What do you do if your initial hypothesis turns out to be completely wrong when you query the data?">
I treat a disproven hypothesis as valuable progress, not failure:

1. **Acknowledge Calmly**: State clearly to the interviewer: *"The data disproves my initial hypothesis: we hypothesized that salary day volume caused the failure surge, but failure rates on the 1st of the month (2.68%) are virtually identical to mid-month baselines (2.71%)."*
2. **Eliminate and Pivot**: Cross off that branch of the hypothesis tree transparently.
3. **Advance to the Next Branch**: Pivot immediately to the next MECE branch: *"Having eliminated date-of-month congestion, I will now investigate partner bank rail stability and time-of-day traffic concentration."*
</InterviewQ>

## Key takeaways

- Never jump straight into SQL; always execute the 6-step framework: Clarify, Structure, Inspect, Analyze, Synthesize, and Recommend.
- Deconstruct the problem using a MECE hypothesis tree before inspecting database tables.
- Perform rapid data hygiene checks (deduplication, string trimming, status filtering) before calculating top-line metrics.
- Format all case deliverables using the executive framework: 1-Line Answer, 3 Quantified Findings, Phased Recommendations, and Risks.
- Practice regularly across our 25 written case studies to build cross-industry intuition and problem-solving confidence.
