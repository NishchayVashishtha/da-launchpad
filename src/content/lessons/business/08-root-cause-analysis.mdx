## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Execute a repeatable 6-step diagnostic protocol when investigating metric shifts: Clarify, Validate Data, Context/Seasonality, Decompose, Test Hypotheses, and Conclude.
- Rule out data pipeline defects, tracking webhook drops, and duplicate logging errors before sounding business alarms.
- Isolate whether a metric drop is systemic across the entire market or concentrated in specific channels, categories, or partner endpoints.
- Calculate segment contribution to change (`Variance Contribution = Delta Segment / Total Delta`) to size driver impacts.
- Deliver a concise, professional 6-sentence response during high-stakes leadership reviews and case interview rounds.

## Why this matters for a Data Analyst job

<RealWorld>
At 9:30 AM on a Monday, an e-commerce country manager sends an all-caps message in Slack: *"Sales dropped 20% in December! Marketing is burning money, our app must be crashing, and competitors are eating our lunch. I need answers by lunch."*

Inexperienced analysts panic. They query random tables, pull thirty different charts, and guess possible reasons: *"Maybe people don't like our new logo?"* or *"Maybe our prices are too high?"* 

A skilled data analyst does not guess. They calmly open a terminal, confirm that December revenue actually grew 7.8% Year-over-Year, demonstrate that every single channel fell identically due to post-Diwali seasonal hangover, and show that cancellations and app crashes remained completely flat. Root Cause Analysis (RCA) is what separates junior dashboard builders from trusted strategic advisors.
</RealWorld>

In virtually every data analyst interview in India, you will face this exact prompt: *"Metric X dropped Y% last week. How would you investigate?"* Interviewers do not care about the final answer; they evaluate your diagnostic discipline.

## Concept

### The 6-Step RCA Diagnostic Framework

When any primary business metric moves unexpectedly, follow this disciplined sequence:

```
[1. Clarify & Define] → [2. Validate Data] → [3. External Context] → [4. Decompose Tree] → [5. Test Hypotheses] → [6. Conclude & Act]
```

#### Step 1: Clarify and Define
Before running a single query, pin down the exact boundaries of the problem:
- **Which specific metric moved?** Does "sales" mean Gross Merchandise Value (GMV) or net delivered revenue?
- **What is the comparison baseline?** Is the drop Week-over-Week (WoW), Month-over-Month (MoM), or Year-over-Year (YoY)?
- **What is the magnitude and timeline?** Did the drop happen overnight on a single Tuesday (incident), or drift downward over 6 weeks (trend)?

#### Step 2: Validate Data Pipeline Hygiene
Never assume the data is correct. Over 40% of sudden metric drops are tracking bugs:
- Did an analytics ingestion job fail or log late-arriving data?
- Was a tracking webhook dropped during a Friday evening deployment?
- Did an anomaly logging error (such as a 100x outlier) corrupt the baseline?

#### Step 3: Check External Context & Seasonality
Determine whether the shift was driven by external environmental factors:
- **Festivals & Holidays**: Did Diwali, Eid, or New Year create an artificial surge in the prior period?
- **Weather & Disruptions**: Did monsoon flooding paralyze home deliveries or clinic visits?
- **Competitor Actions**: Did a major competitor run a 50% cashback flash sale?

#### Step 4: Decompose via Metric Tree
Deconstruct the metric using structured trees:
- **Multiplicative Driver Cut**: Did Revenue fall because Orders dropped, or because Average Order Value (AOV) contracted?
- **Additive Segment Cuts**: Cut across Platforms (Android, iOS, Web), Payment Methods (COD, UPI, Cards), Product Categories, and Geographies.

#### Step 5: Formulate and Test Hypotheses
Rank potential explanations by magnitude and test each with one targeted SQL query. Compare the explained gap against total variance.

#### Step 6: Conclude, Quantify Impact, and Recommend
Synthesize the findings into an executive summary:
1. What happened (with numbers).
2. Root cause identified with evidence.
3. Recommended business remedy and revenue recovery estimate.

<FormulaCard title="Contribution to Total Change">
To evaluate whether a segment explains the bulk of a drop, calculate its contribution share:
`Contribution to Change = (Delta Segment Volume / Total Platform Delta Volume) * 100`

If total revenue fell by Rs 532,014 and Android revenue fell by Rs 150,000, Android contributed 28.2% of the drop. If no single segment accounts for more than its natural volume share, the drop is systemic across the entire market.
</FormulaCard>

## Worked examples

All examples in this section query `public/data/sqlite/ecommerce.sqlite` and `fintech.sqlite` using SQLite.

### Example 1: Full RCA on DesiCart's December Performance
Leadership reports that December sales collapsed after a record November. We apply the 6-step framework.

#### Step 1 & 2: Clarify & Validate Data
We query distinct orders, filter out extreme logging outliers (`total_amount < 100000`), and check delivered performance:

```sql
WITH o AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT * FROM orders
  WHERE total_amount < 100000 AND order_status = 'Delivered'
)
SELECT
  strftime('%Y-%m', order_date) AS month,
  COUNT(*) AS delivered_orders,
  CAST(ROUND(SUM(total_amount)) AS INTEGER) AS revenue,
  ROUND(AVG(total_amount), 2) AS aov
FROM o
WHERE strftime('%Y-%m', order_date) IN ('2024-11', '2024-12')
GROUP BY month
ORDER BY month ASC;
```

**Real Output from ecommerce.sqlite:**
| month | delivered_orders | revenue | aov |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 2024-11 | 496 | 2591559 | 5224.92 |
| 2024-12 | 423 | 2059545 | 4868.90 |

**Finding:** Delivered revenue fell by -20.53% (-Rs 532,014), and delivered orders fell by -14.72% (-73 orders). Both volume and basket size contracted.

#### Step 3: Check Seasonality & YoY Performance
Was December 2024 genuinely weak, or was November an abnormal festive peak? We compare December YoY:

```sql
WITH o AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT * FROM orders
  WHERE total_amount < 100000
)
SELECT
  strftime('%Y', order_date) AS year,
  COUNT(*) AS orders,
  CAST(ROUND(SUM(CASE WHEN order_status = 'Delivered' THEN total_amount ELSE 0 END)) AS INTEGER) AS revenue
FROM o
WHERE strftime('%m', order_date) = '12' AND strftime('%Y', order_date) IN ('2023', '2024')
GROUP BY year
ORDER BY year ASC;
```

**Real Output from ecommerce.sqlite:**
| year | orders | revenue |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 2023 | 434 | 1910326 |
| 2024 | 458 | 2059545 |

**Finding:** Year-over-Year, December 2024 orders grew by +5.5% (458 vs 434) and delivered revenue grew by +7.8% (Rs 20.6 Lakh vs Rs 19.1 Lakh). November 2024 was artificially elevated by Diwali shopping (which extended into the first week of November). In 2023, November to December orders also contracted (-2.7%). The drop was a normal post-festive seasonal hangover, not a business failure.

#### Step 4: Decompose by Channel
Did a technical bug break one specific app channel?

```sql
WITH o AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT * FROM orders
  WHERE total_amount < 100000
)
SELECT
  sales_channel,
  SUM(CASE WHEN strftime('%Y-%m', order_date) = '2024-11' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS nov_orders,
  SUM(CASE WHEN strftime('%Y-%m', order_date) = '2024-12' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS dec_orders,
  ROUND((SUM(CASE WHEN strftime('%Y-%m', order_date) = '2024-12' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) - SUM(CASE WHEN strftime('%Y-%m', order_date) = '2024-11' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)) * 100.0 / SUM(CASE WHEN strftime('%Y-%m', order_date) = '2024-11' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END), 1) AS change_pct
FROM o
WHERE strftime('%Y-%m', order_date) IN ('2024-11', '2024-12')
GROUP BY sales_channel
ORDER BY change_pct ASC;
```

**Real Output from ecommerce.sqlite:**
| sales_channel | nov_orders | dec_orders | change_pct |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Website | 109 | 86 | -21.1 |
| iOS App | 98 | 79 | -19.4 |
| Mobile Web | 64 | 54 | -15.6 |
| Android App | 262 | 239 | -8.8 |

**Finding:** Every single channel fell between -8.8% and -21.1%. Order cancellations remained completely flat (27 in November vs 27 in December). The drop was market-wide demand contraction, completely ruling out an isolated mobile app release bug.

#### Step 5 & 6: Conclude and Recommend
- **Conclusion**: The MoM revenue drop of -20.5% was driven by natural post-Diwali festive hangover coupled with fewer promotional coupon redemptions (110 in Nov vs 77 in Dec). YoY growth remains healthy at +7.8%.
- **Action**: Calibrate financial targets to Year-over-Year growth benchmarks rather than festive peaks. For December 2025, plan targeted year-end clearance campaigns to stimulate post-festive buying.

## Common mistakes

<CommonMistake>
- **Jumping to conclusions before sizing the problem**: Suggesting an expensive loyalty revamp before verifying whether the drop is Rs 50,000 or Rs 50 Lakh.
- **Comparing against an abnormal festive baseline**: Treating a post-Diwali month as a normal operational period without checking annual seasonality.
- **Skipping data pipeline validation**: Spending three days diagnosing a customer churn spike when an engineering team simply deployed an unverified tracking tag.
- **Single-segment tunnel vision**: Focusing exclusively on a 15% drop in Bangalore while missing that the entire national market fell 18%.
- **Delivering observations without recommendations**: Presenting 15 charts showing that sales dropped, without telling leadership what concrete action to take.
</CommonMistake>

## Interview angle

<InterviewAlert>
"Sales dropped 15%—how would you investigate?" is the most common diagnostic case question in tech interviews. Frame your answer using the structured 6-step framework rather than rattling off random guesses.
</InterviewAlert>

<InterviewQ q="Sales dropped 15% last week. How would you investigate?">
I investigate metric drops using a structured 6-step diagnostic protocol:
1. **Clarify & Define**: Confirm whether sales means gross orders or delivered revenue, verify the exact dates, and check whether the 15% drop is Week-over-Week or Year-over-Year.
2. **Validate Data Pipeline**: Check with data engineering whether tracking webhooks, payment logs, or attribution pipelines experienced downtime or data loss.
3. **External & Seasonal Context**: Check the calendar for public holidays, festive hangovers, severe weather disruptions, or competitor promotional campaigns.
4. **Decompose via Metric Tree**: Decompose Sales into Orders $\times$ Average Order Value. Then segment additively by platform (Android vs iOS vs Web), channel, and product category.
5. **Formulate & Test Hypotheses**: If orders fell, test whether checkout payment failure rates spiked, bestselling SKUs were out of stock, or ad spend was paused.
6. **Synthesize & Recommend**: Quantify the verified root cause in rupees and deliver an action plan to restore volume.
</InterviewQ>

<InterviewQ q="Payment success rate dropped from 97.5% to 96.5% overnight. How do you triage the issue in SQL?">
I execute an immediate 4-step triage sequence:
1. **Cut by Issuing Bank**: Group by `bank` and compare success rates against 7-day rolling baselines. If Kaveri Co-op Bank dropped to 85%, the issue is an external banking host timeout.
2. **Cut by Client App**: Group by `payment_app`. If only NammaPay dropped, check recent client app deployments for an SDK networking regression.
3. **Inspect Failure Codes**: Group by `failure_code` to see if the decline is technical (`BANK_SERVER_TIMEOUT`, `UPI_SYSTEM_DOWN`) or user-side (`INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`).
4. **Hourly Incident Window**: Group by hour to pinpoint the exact minute the drop began and notify on-call infrastructure engineers.
</InterviewQ>

<InterviewQ q="What is the difference between an observation and an insight in an RCA report?">
An **observation** simply describes the raw data without context: *"December sales dropped 20.5% and fashion returns were 20.2%."*

An **insight** explains the underlying mechanism, business significance, and operational action: *"December's 20.5% revenue drop was a normal post-Diwali seasonal hangover, while Year-over-Year sales expanded +7.8%. Meanwhile, fashion returns of 20.2% are driven primarily by size mismatches (53% of returns); introducing an interactive size finder will recover an estimated Rs 8 Lakh in lost margin."*
</InterviewQ>

## Key takeaways

- Never guess causes; follow a structured 6-step framework from clarification to data validation, context, decomposition, testing, and action.
- Always check Year-over-Year (YoY) benchmarks to avoid mistaking normal post-festive seasonal hangovers for operational crises.
- Over 40% of sudden metric drops originate in broken tracking webhooks, ingestion job timeouts, or outlier logging errors.
- If a metric drop affects all client platforms and channels uniformly, it is driven by broad consumer demand rather than a localized software defect.
- Quantify segment impact using contribution to change (`Delta Segment / Total Delta`) to focus business remediation where leverage is highest.
