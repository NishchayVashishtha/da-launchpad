## Learning objectives

- Describe what you will learn in each of the 12 weeks.
- Use the day-by-day structure of weeks 1 to 4, including the revision and mock day.
- Choose a version of the plan that fits your daily hours.
- Know what to cut first when you fall behind.

## Why this matters for a Data Analyst job

A plan turns a huge topic list into small daily tasks. Without it, most learners jump between videos, feel busy and still cannot write a join query in an interview. The plan is built around what fresher analyst interviews actually test: SQL first, then Excel, a BI tool, some Python, statistics and business thinking, and finally projects and interviews.

<RealWorld>
An analyst team lead in Hyderabad said: "In a fresher interview, I ask for two SQL queries, one Excel task and one 'what would you check?' question. People who practised daily for two months handle all four calmly." A good plan trains exactly these four things.
</RealWorld>

## Concept

### The shape of the 12 weeks

The plan assumes **5 to 6 hours a day, 6 days a week**, with day 7 for revision and a mock test. Open the Dashboard and press "Start plan today". It shows the task for each day and links to the lesson.

| Weeks | Main focus | What you can do by the end |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set up tools, Excel basics, first SQL queries | Clean a small sheet, run SELECT and WHERE queries |
| 2 | Excel formulas and pivots, SQL filtering and aggregates | Build a pivot report, use GROUP BY |
| 3 | Excel lookups and charts, SQL joins | Join tables and answer two-table questions |
| 4 | SQL subqueries and CTEs, Excel dashboard | Write multi-step queries, finish an Excel dashboard |
| 5 | SQL window functions, Power BI begins | Rank, running totals; load data in Power BI |
| 6 | Power BI modelling and DAX, Python begins; start applying | A first dashboard, Python basics, resume ready |
| 7 | Python pandas; Project 1 | Clean and analyse a dataset with code |
| 8 | Python visualisation, statistics begins | Charts with code, mean, spread, distributions |
| 9 | Statistics (testing, A/B), business case thinking; Project 2 | Read an A/B test, structure a case answer |
| 10 | Project 3, SQL interview patterns | Finish a portfolio-grade project |
| 11 | Interview preparation, project polish, applications | Strong GitHub and a rehearsed story |
| 12 | Final mock week | Full timed mocks and gap fixing |

The key idea: **SQL starts on day 2 and continues for five weeks**, because it is the most tested skill. Excel runs alongside it early. Applications begin from week 6, so you learn the real market while you still study.

### The daily rhythm (5 to 6 hours)

A good day has four blocks. Take a short break between blocks.

1. **Learn (1.5 to 2 hours).** One lesson or concept, typed along, not only watched.
2. **Practise (2 hours).** Practice questions on the new topic and yesterday's topic.
3. **Build or apply (1 to 1.5 hours).** Work on a mini project, a dashboard, or job applications.
4. **Review (30 to 45 minutes).** Flashcards, an error log, and writing three lines about what you learned.

### Weeks 1 to 4: day by day

Each day of the first four weeks has an entry on your Dashboard. Day 7 of every week is for **revision and a mock**: redo your wrong questions, then take a short timed test of 30 to 45 minutes. This day is not optional. Weekly mocks show you what you forgot before an interviewer does.

### Weeks 5 to 12: week by week

After week 4 you know how to learn, so the plan gives a weekly target instead of daily steps. You decide the daily split. The Dashboard shows the weekly entry from the first day of that week until the next one.

### Adjusting for your hours

The plan is built for 6 hours. If you have less time, **do not skip SQL practice**. Stretch the timeline instead of dropping topics.

| Your hours per day | Total length | How to adapt |
|---|---|---|
| 6 hours | 12 weeks | Follow the plan as written. |
| 4 hours | About 16 to 18 weeks | Take 1.5 weeks for each plan week. Keep the Learn and Practise blocks, move Build time to weekends, and combine Review into the evening. Start applications after SQL and one BI tool are done. |
| 2 hours | About 6 months | Study in one block: 45 minutes learn, 60 minutes practise, 15 minutes review. Do one topic at a time in this order: SQL, Excel, Power BI, Python. Do one small project. Use weekends for longer sessions of 4 hours. |

If you also have college classes, count only focused hours. A 2-hour focused day beats 5 hours with your phone beside you.

<Tip>
When you fall behind, cut in this order: extra videos first, then Python plotting depth, then Tableau comparison, then statistics depth. Never cut SQL practice, mock tests or your projects.
</Tip>

### Checkpoints

Use these to see whether you are on track:

- End of week 2: you can write GROUP BY with a filter without looking up syntax.
- End of week 4: you can solve two-table join questions in under 10 minutes.
- End of week 6: you have one Power BI dashboard and a resume ready for applications.
- End of week 9: you have finished one project end to end.
- End of week 12: you can pass a timed 5-question SQL test.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: A good day in week 3

| Time | Block | Task |
|---|---|---|
| 9:00 to 11:00 | Learn | SQL INNER JOIN lesson, type every example |
| 11:15 to 13:15 | Practise | 8 join questions plus 4 WHERE and GROUP BY questions from last week |
| 14:30 to 16:00 | Build | Excel lookup exercise on a sales sheet |
| 16:15 to 17:00 | Review | Flashcards, update error log, write a three-line summary |

### Example 2: Converting to a 4-hour schedule

Original week 3 content: joins, Excel lookups, charts. With 4 hours you give it 10 to 11 days. Days 1 to 3: SQL joins and practice. Days 4 to 5: Excel lookups. Days 6 to 7: charts and revision. Days 8 to 10: mixed practice and a mock. Then move on to week 4. The order stays the same and nothing is dropped.

### Example 3: Recovering after a missed week

You were ill for 5 days. Do not try to catch up by studying 12 hours a day. Instead, restart from the last topic you understood, redo one old test to find gaps, and move your end date by one week. Consistency matters more than speed.

## Common mistakes

<CommonMistake>
Spending the first two weeks only on setup and theory. Write your first SQL query on day 2.
</CommonMistake>

<CommonMistake>
Skipping day 7 revision because "there is too much to cover". Without revision, new topics push out old ones.
</CommonMistake>

<CommonMistake>
Waiting until week 12 to apply for jobs. You lose the chance to learn from real interviews.
</CommonMistake>

<CommonMistake>
Copying the plan for 6 hours when you only have 3. You will quit by week 3. Pick a realistic version and stay consistent.
</CommonMistake>

<CommonMistake>
Changing plans every week after watching a new roadmap video. Choose one plan and finish it.
</CommonMistake>

## Interview angle

<InterviewAlert>
"How did you prepare?" is a common question. A structured plan with projects and mocks is a strong, believable answer.
</InterviewAlert>

<InterviewQ q="How did you prepare for this role?">
I followed a 12-week plan with 5 to 6 hours a day. I focused first on SQL and Excel, then Power BI and Python basics, and built three projects. Each week ended with a timed mock test, and I kept an error log to revise mistakes. I began applying in week 6 to learn from real interviews.
</InterviewQ>

<InterviewQ q="How do you manage your time when learning something new?">
I break the topic into small daily goals, split each day into learn, practise, build and review blocks, and use weekly checkpoints. If I fall behind, I protect the high-value items and shift the timeline rather than skipping practice.
</InterviewQ>

## Key takeaways

- The plan follows what interviews test: SQL first, then Excel, BI, Python, statistics, business thinking, projects and interviews.
- Weeks 1 to 4 are daily; weeks 5 to 12 are weekly targets.
- Day 7 is for revision and a mock, not optional.
- For 4 or 2 hours a day, stretch the timeline and keep the order.
- Start applying around week 6 and never cut SQL practice.
