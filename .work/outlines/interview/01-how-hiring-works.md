## Concept (teaching order)
- Analogy: hiring is a funnel of filters; each round removes one kind of risk (can't code SQL, can't think, can't communicate, won't stay).
- Stages table (use markdown table): Resume screen/ATS (2 min read, keywords+projects) -> Online assessment (aptitude + SQL/MCQ, 45-90 min, auto-scored cut-off) -> Live SQL/Excel/Power BI test (30-60 min) -> Take-home (2-5 days, 1-4 h real work) -> Technical interview (45 min: SQL, stats, projects) -> Case round (30-45 min: business problem) -> Manager round (fit, ownership, project depth) -> HR round (salary, notice, relocation, bond, documents).
- Which companies use which: fintech/startups = take-home + live SQL + case; IT services/GCC = aptitude + technical + HR; consulting = case-heavy + aptitude; campus drives = OA + 1-2 interviews.
- Typical timelines: campus 1 day; services 2-3 weeks; fintech startup 2-4 weeks; take-home adds 1 week. Offer to joining 1-6 months.
- Funnel numbers (illustrative, say "typical, varies"): 100 applications -> 10-15 screens -> 4-6 interviews -> 1 offer. Do not state as fact from a source.
- What each round tests (one line) + how to prepare (link to lessons: SQL module, statistics, 03 case, 04 take-home, 06 live test, 07 salary).
- Where freshers fail: OA cut-off, SQL under time, can't explain own project, vague "why DA", salary panic.

## Worked examples (no SqlPlayground needed; 3 mini-walkthroughs in prose/tables)
1. A fintech startup process, day by day (Day 1 apply, Day 3 recruiter call, Day 5 SQL test, Day 8 take-home, Day 14 review, Day 18 manager, Day 21 HR, Day 25 offer).
2. IT-services campus drive: OA (aptitude 60%/SQL 40%) -> tech -> HR in one day.
3. Reading a job post: mark must-have skills vs nice-to-have; map to modules on this site.
## Common mistakes
- Preparing only SQL, then failing aptitude cut-off -> rejected before a human sees you.
- Ignoring the HR round -> offer revoked over vague answers on notice/relocation.
- Treating take-home as homework, not a work sample -> messy submission.
- Applying to 5 firms only -> no data to learn from; apply 10 a day (link to job-search).
- Not asking recruiter what the rounds are -> blind prep.
## Quiz (5)
1 Which round auto-rejects by cut-off? OA (traps: HR, manager, case). 2 Take-home mostly tests? Structure+judgment, not complexity (traps: speed, fancy ML, long code). 3 What does the case round test? Structured thinking on a business problem. 4 Best first reply when recruiter calls? Ask about rounds and timeline. 5 HR round risk? Mismatch on salary/notice/relocation/bond, not SQL.
## Interview Qs (3)
- Walk me through what you expect in a DA hiring process. (key: stages and prep per stage)
- Why do companies use take-home tests? (work sample, reduces bluff)
- How do you prepare when you don't know the rounds? (ask recruiter, read Glassdoor-like sources generically, default to SQL+case+HR)
## Takeaways: 8 stages; OA is a filter; know company type; ask rounds; each round = one risk.
